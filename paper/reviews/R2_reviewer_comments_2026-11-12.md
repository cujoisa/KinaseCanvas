## Reviewer 2

> The authors have carefully considered my comments and this is reflected in the revised manuscript which is
> close to ready for publication. I believe that the KinaseCANVAS website represents a very important resource
> for the protein phosphorylation research community.

**G1 (cross-species conservation).**

> One question that I did not consider previously was whether the existence of a kinase-substrate relationship
> not necessarily identified in human data, may exist in results from kinases and their substrates in other
> animal models. It seems from page 24/5219, third paragraph, that it does not. If these kinases and their
> substrates are highly conserved (especially the phosphorylation site in the substrate), it could be useful if
> Kinase Canvas use this information in the future for prediction in a human context. This refinement might be
> suggested at the end of the manuscript in terms of future directions.

**G2 (stimulus-dependent co-regulation).**

> Another criteria that might be applied in future studies is whether diverse stimuli in vivo correlate with the
> activation of the kinase and increased phosphorylation of the phosphosite of the substrate pair. Such
> information is partly available from PhosphositePlus, but unfortunately the directionality of the effect (i.e.,
> increased or decreased) is not provided. Some low quality (antibody microarray) information in this regard
> might be available from www.kinet-am.ca. On page 34/5219 lines 46-48, this appears to be briefly mentioned, but
> perhaps the authors can expand on this.

**G3 (in vitro validation).**

> I am not suggesting that the following is necessary for the publication, but it would have strengthen the
> publication if some examples of the novel predicted substrates of certain kinases could have been confirmed in
> vitro with purified proteins and Mg•ATP. If some 20 predicted KSI were tested, it would be interesting to know
> how many of them turned out to be true, at least in vitro.
